<section>
  <h2>June, July, August</h2>
  <p>Peak season exists for real reasons. Newport in summer is genuinely exceptional: harbor light, warm evenings, and a town that knows exactly what it is doing. If the budget supports it and the venue is locked, a July or August Saturday in Newport is hard to beat.</p>
  <div className="article-card-grid sm:grid-cols-2">
    <div className="article-card"><p className="article-card-title">What peak season gives you</p><p>Long days, strong outdoor ceremony weather, maximum destination energy, and full local vendor availability.</p></div>
    <div className="article-card"><p className="article-card-title">What peak season costs you</p><p>Higher venue fees, expensive hotel blocks, heavier traffic, parking pressure, and event-calendar landmines.</p></div>
  </div>
  <p>June is the gentlest of the three months. If peak season is the goal and the budget allows, a June Saturday is often the most manageable version of it.</p>
</section>

<section>
  <h2>September</h2>
  <p>September is the best month to get married in Newport. The crowds thin after Labor Day, the light gets better, temperatures are ideal, hotel rates soften, and premium vendors suddenly have weekends open.</p>
  <div className="article-highlight">
    <p className="article-highlight-title">The only caveat</p>
    <p>Early September weekends that fall on or near Labor Day can still carry summer pricing and summer hotel demand. The real shoulder-season break tends to happen after the second week of September.</p>
  </div>
</section>

<section>
  <h2>October</h2>
  <p>October in Newport is beautiful in a different way: less about harbor energy and more about atmosphere, color, and a quieter version of the place. Estate properties look exceptional and the town is easier to move through.</p>
  <p>The tradeoff is weather variability. Sunset is earlier, wind picks up, and waterfront formats need stronger contingencies than they do in September. October works best for venues with strong indoor space.</p>
</section>

<section>
  <h2>November and December</h2>
  <p>Late fall Newport works for couples who want an intimate, indoor-leaning wedding with a lower budget than peak season requires. It does not work as well for couples chasing the full Newport destination-weekend atmosphere.</p>
  <p>December is especially narrow. Some venues close, and those that stay open can be charming, but vendor and venue availability need to be confirmed before building a plan around the date.</p>
</section>

<section>
  <h2>April and May</h2>
  <p>Spring offers better pricing and better vendor access, but the weather is less reliable in a way that matters emotionally as well as logistically. If the design depends on outdoor space, spring requires an indoor backup you are genuinely comfortable using.</p>
  <p>May is the stronger of the two months. April is more of a gamble unless the venue's indoor option is truly strong.</p>
</section>
